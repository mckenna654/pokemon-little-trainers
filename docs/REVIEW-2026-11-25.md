# FireRed and Little Trainers review — 25 November 2026

## Verdict

Little Trainers has a sound direction for a non-reading preschool player: keep Kanto and the child's choices, remove punishment, and show a route forward. Claude's existing gameplay work should continue on the current foundation. The largest remaining obstacles are original text-heavy interfaces and interactions, followed by consistent artwork and hands-on testing.

This is a broad **source and design review**, not a fresh play-through, a security audit, or a claim that every possible game state works. It covers the original game's systems relevant to this adaptation, all 33 patches as a reconstructed comparison, the current UI artwork, tests and roadmap. Source reviewed: project commit `451c71a`, pinned upstream `ce6406d8c2eaf46d543c8a2606de0c3cb1951982`. The patches apply cleanly to that pin; the change spans 49 game files. The linked Claude web session could not be read through the available web tool, so its uncommitted work is outside this review.

The target in `AGENTS.md` is ages **3–6**. Children younger than three are outside the documented/tested target. Adult-assisted play is a reasonable expectation until controls, icon understanding, pacing and wandering have been observed with the intended children.

## Findings for Claude, in priority order

### 1. Protect the remaining permanent-loss flows

**High priority; source-confirmed gap against the specification.** Neither `src/pokemon_storage_system_data.c` nor `src/item_menu.c` is modified by the 33 patches. `ReleaseMon()` still reaches `PurgeMonOrBoxMon()`; the bag's `Task_ConfirmTossItems()` and `Task_WaitAB_RedrawAndReturnToBag()` still lead to `RemoveBagItem()`. Other original protections may prevent particular releases or tossing key items, but there is no general Little Trainer guard here.

Recommendation: on Little, remove destructive choices or gate them behind a parent-only flow; inspect selling, trading and new-game/save replacement too. Add tests that actually enter these menus and try repeated A presses. The current “nothing lost for good” principle is a design target, not a verified property of the whole game.

### 2. Finish the screens reached by the new pictures

**High priority; already acknowledged in the roadmap.** CHANGE enters the original party UI. Bag, shops, storage and Pokédex remain substantial reading tasks. The Start-menu pictures are only 14 pixels inside 16-pixel cells.

Recommendation: make party choice the next development slice, then inventory/storage. Ask a child to choose another healthy creature without prompting. Use large source illustrations from this pack when redesigning layouts; do not enlarge the current sheet in place.

### 3. Separate easier gameplay from gentler story content

**Parent-facing decision, not an automatic censorship request.** The untouched `data/maps/PokemonTower_1F/text.inc`, `PokemonTower_2F/text.inc` and `PokemonTower_6F/text.inc` retain death/ghost material. `CeladonCity_GameCorner/scripts.inc` and its text retain coin and slot-machine interactions.

Recommendation: Jake should choose whether to retain, soften or bypass these themes on Little. A complete preschool edition needs an intentional story/tone pass as well as an easier battle system.

### 4. Make useful and unavailable actions predictable

**Medium priority.** `SlotShown()` and `SlotPosition()` compact battle buttons when SPECIAL or CHANGE is unavailable. This avoids dead choices, but moves CATCH/RUN around. A creature with only one usable move gets a generic ATTACK burst rather than the type picture shown for SPECIAL. When capture storage is full, failure is only a sound. Trapping effects can still lead to a rejected switch after entering the party screen.

Recommendation: test recognition and positional memory with children. Consider stable reserved positions and a clear visual unavailable state; keep actions purposeful. Fix storage-full feedback and the trapped-switch route separately from the art.

### 5. Keep the scope of assistance and saving honest

**Medium priority.** Junior keeps original puzzles although it has an arrow. Auto-save is tied to advancing story goals in `LT_Nav_Update()`; it does not protect every optional capture. Switching to Classic after Little has opened permanent puzzle flags does not reconstruct the original puzzle state.

Recommendation: document mode changes as assistance changes, not restoring an untouched Classic save. Test long wandering, save/reload after optional captures, and parent mode switching. Consider a visible save cue that does not add pressure.

### 6. Verify presentation on compiled screens

**Medium priority.** Issue #1 showed that extensive memory-reading tests missed visible arrow, palette, duplicate-button and window defects. The new icon pack preserves contracts, but this does not prove its compiled appearance. Animated-ring index 14, shared menu palettes, sprite frame ordering and title placement are especially sensitive.

Recommendation: integrate the pack, preserve it in the generator pipeline, compile, take the screen tour and inspect every relevant scene. The new title is a subtitle plaque; it does not replace FireRed's complete title composition or animation.

### 7. Preserve good engineering decisions

The pinned base, modification-only repository, central preset rules, automatic patch replay, explicit test evidence and prohibition on ROM uploads are useful foundations. Continue incremental work; no engine migration is justified by the artwork request. Keep gameplay development with Claude and record accepted generated art under `assets/`.

Follow-up engineering checks: test navigation queue capacity/path timing on large maps, review status-protection exceptions rather than assuming Safeguard means universal immunity, and test save interruptions/failure handling. These are review suggestions, not newly reproduced faults.

## Artwork delivered by this review

[Generated UI pack](../assets/generated/v1/README.md): **27 newly generated transparent source images** — 21 battle icons, five menu illustrations and one title plaque. It includes nine GBA-format sheets, current palettes, optional 32-pixel menu pictures, exact prompts, hashes and before/after previews.

Clearer representations include a friendly head for CHANGE, a feather for Flying, a blue dragon head rather than a second flame, and distinct rock/earth silhouettes. Exact control marks remain the existing original pixel art. No third-party tileset was imported and no gameplay patch was changed.
